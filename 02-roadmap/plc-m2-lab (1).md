# Prioritization & Roadmapping, Module 2 Lab

## Prioritize your Rocks and your Hard Nos
- **Rock #1:** Create a foreman-facing mobile app. Supports KR1’s increase in sustained adoption from 15% to 60% and KR3’s increase in cases resolved without duplicate reporting or entry from 25% to 70%. Clear ownership and returned answers also support KR2’s resolution-time target.
- **Rock #2:** Build an offline-first mobile experience, Supports KR1 by making the workflow dependable on job sites and KR3 by reducing the need to repeat reports through another channel or re-enter them after a shift.
- **Rock #3:** Add photo markup and annotation. Supports KR2’s reduction in median resolution time from 24 to 12 hours by making reports clearer and reducing clarification exchanges. Easier capture also supports KR1.
- **❌ Hard No #1:** Executive reporting dashboard with custom KPIs. executive reporting does not directly improve field teams’ ability to capture, route, or resolve blockers.
- **❌ Hard No #2:** Time-tracking module, time tracking introduces a separate workflow and substantial scope without directly advancing the blocker-resolution OKRs.
- **❌ Hard No #3:** Refresh the web UI, does not address the mobile reliability, clear ownership, and duplicate reporting problems identified in our strategy.

## Show and swap
- **Do the Rock selections feel traceable to a clear strategy, or do they read like a feature list?:** They read as traceable, and the tell is that none of the three justifications stop at "this is impactful." Each one names a specific piece of the strategy it's discharging: Rock #1 (foreman mobile app) is tied to a where-to-play decision (a lightweight, purpose-built mobile view, separate from the PM web app); Rock #2 (offline-first) is tied to the winning aspiration itself, since "receive the answer the moment it exists" is meaningless if the app dies at a signal-dead job site; Rock #3 (AI-assisted RFI drafting) is tied to a named world-class capability (routing accuracy) and explicitly argued as the mechanism that makes that capability achievable rather than a feature that sounds related to it.
- **Pick one Hard No and make the case for why it should actually be a Rock.:** I'd take Hard No #2, the automated compliance checklist generator. The case for promoting it: it's the only one of the three Hard Nos with a concrete, near-term revenue signal attached to it that isn't itself the objection - three actual clients, via enterprise sales, are asking for this now, which is a firmer footing than "we might lose two renewals" (Hard No #1) or "nothing forces the question" (Hard No #3, by its own admission). More importantly, it doesnt require opening a new direction the way the executive dashboard does. Rock #3 is already building a structured-data capture pipeline out of RFI drafting; a compliance checklist is arguably just a second output of that same pipeline, not a new one. If the capture engine is the moat, shipping a second consumer of that same clean data could be marginal effort compared to building it from scratch later, and it converts three specific at-risk clients into a reason to invest in the capture engine harder, rather than a distraction from it.
